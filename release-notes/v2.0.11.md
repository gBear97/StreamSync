StreamSync 2.0.11 pauses your film when the stream pauses, and plays it again from exactly where the stream is.

## What's new

- **Pause with the stream.** A new switch on the main window, under **Keep in sync automatically**. When the streamer pauses the film, your copy pauses, usually within a second or two. When the film comes back on the stream, yours plays from exactly where the stream is, usually about a second later and within a hundredth of a second of it. It starts working after your first sync. It replaces auto mode's "Follow the stream's pauses", which checked every 30 s and so noticed a pause half a minute or more late.
- **Only the film's own sound decides.** Ten times a second, StreamSync compares the stream's last 0.6 s with your film at the place the stream should be. Where the film has sound and the stream doesn't carry it, the film pauses, whatever the streamer is saying. Where the film's own track is quiet, there's nothing to listen for and it plays on. The line under the switch says what it hears: **Hearing the film**, **Film is quiet here** or **Stream paused**.
- **A streamer talking through the pause doesn't restart your film.** Their voice doesn't count as the film coming back, even where it happens to resemble the film's sound. In live tests the film stayed paused through more than a minute of talk at a time.
- **It finds the stream if it comes back somewhere else.** A stream that returns up to about 2 s from where it stopped is picked up as quickly as one that returns in place. If the streamer went back a bit or jumped to another scene, StreamSync searches outward from where it stopped, and in time the whole film, and plays from there.
- **Short pauses leave your windows alone.** With **Show the stream while the film is paused** on, a pause that Pause with the stream makes brings the stream's browser forward only once it has lasted 2 seconds. Most of its pauses are shorter than that: the film holds for a second and plays on, still in fullscreen if it was. A pause you make yourself shows the stream at once, as before.
- **Choose how soon it pauses.** **Settings > Pause with the stream** offers **Balanced** (the default), **Fast**, **Instant** and **Steady**. The faster ones also pause for a moment more often while a streamer talks over the film.
- **Lip sync is held within a tenth of a second.** Auto mode's checks and Pause with the stream now move the film once it's 0.1 s off the stream. It used to take 0.35 s, and a browser's sound can slip by less than that over an evening.
- **A first sync into a big film lands where it should.** A very large file can take a few seconds to start playing the first time. The player gave up waiting after 3 s and left the film that far behind. Now it waits up to 15 s.

## Good to know

- **A streamer talking loudly over the film can hide it for a moment.** On Balanced that made about 9 to 12 short pauses an hour in live tests, each about a second, after which the film plays on in sync. They no longer move your windows.
- **A stop that comes a second or two after the film has just restarted** can be missed for a few seconds. The film then runs ahead, and one short pause puts it back.
- **Steady** makes the fewest false pauses, but it can take much longer to notice a stop while the streamer talks, and can miss one.
- **A stream you mute sounds the same as a paused one,** so the film pauses then too.
- **On a Mac, the recording indicator stays on** while the switch is on and the film is synced, because StreamSync listens all the time.

## Updating

- **From 2.0.x:** accept the update offer in the app, or use **Settings > Check now**. Your settings are kept. Pause with the stream starts on, unless you had switched "Follow the stream's pauses" off.
- **From 1.x:** follow the "Upgrading from 1.x" steps in the [2.0.0 release notes](https://github.com/gBear97/StreamSync/releases/tag/v2.0.0). On Windows that means installing the setup program once by hand.
