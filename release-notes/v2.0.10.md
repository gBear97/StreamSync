StreamSync 2.0.10 makes syncs exact to a few milliseconds, and can line the film up with the stream's picture instead of its sound.

## What's new

- **Syncs are exact to a few milliseconds.** A sync's search places the stream's sound in 16 ms steps, and the player used to stop correcting once the film was within 20 ms. Now each match is made exact against the film's own sound, to a fraction of a millisecond, and the player corrects down to 8 ms. If the recording wasn't clear enough for that (a quiet scene, or a voice over the film), StreamSync listens 10 s more, up to three times, and moves the film by what it hears. A move of more than 50 ms means the stream itself moved, so StreamSync makes it only once a second listen agrees.
- **On Windows, the film's picture now lands on the stream's sound.** Measured against a live Twitch stream, it ran 18 ms ahead. StreamSync dated the stream's sound by when it arrived, about 20 ms before the speakers play it. Now it goes by when Windows plays it, as the Mac already did. And the film window shows each frame a little after the player's clock reaches it, about 13 ms on a 144 Hz screen. After each sync with the built-in player, StreamSync now times that and runs the film that much ahead. **Settings > Picture** shows the figure.
- **Line the film up with the stream's picture, for watching side by side.** A stream's picture can run behind its own sound: 142 ms on one Twitch stream, the same on every computer watching, because it happens before the stream reaches you. Next to the stream's window, the film then looks that far ahead, though it's right on the voices. The new **Picture** section in Settings offers **The stream's sound** (the default) or **The stream's picture**. The picture choice puts the film later by what **Measure** found for that stream, so the film's lips trail the voices you hear by as much.
- **Measure times a stream's picture against its sound (Windows).** While the stream shows the film, press **Measure** under **Settings > Picture**. StreamSync brings up the stream's window, the one picked under **Settings > Stream window**, and times the film's scene cuts in it against the stream's sound: for 20 s, or up to a minute in a film of long takes. Keep the window uncovered meanwhile. If it was minimized, it's minimized again after. Each stream's figure is kept by its window's title (such as `somechannel - Twitch`) and shown under Picture. The screen grabs stay in memory and only the figures are kept: no picture is saved or sent. A Mac has the choice but not Measure yet, because grabbing the screen there takes Screen Recording permission, which StreamSync no longer asks for. So on a Mac the film stays on the sound.
- **Fine-tune steps by a frame.** The **−0.1** and **+0.1** buttons are now **−1 frame** and **+1 frame**: one of the film's own frames, 42 ms at 24 fps. A film a frame early or late is what you notice side by side. The 0.5 s steps stay.

## Updating

- **From 2.0.x:** accept the update offer in the app, or use **Settings > Check now**. Your settings are kept, and the film goes on lining up with the stream's sound unless you choose its picture under **Settings > Picture**.
- **From 1.x:** follow the "Upgrading from 1.x" steps in the [2.0.0 release notes](https://github.com/gBear97/StreamSync/releases/tag/v2.0.0). On Windows that means installing the setup program once by hand.
