the idea

Watch-along streams are great company and terrible picture.

The stream gives you the part that matters — the streamer's jokes, gasps and hot takes. But the film arrives squeezed to 720p, buried behind a facecam, five settings darker than it should be. You already own a better copy. StreamSync lets the stream carry the voices while your own file carries the picture — and keeps the two locked together all night.

how a sync happens

Eight seconds from click to locked.

T+0.0 s

Listen

One click records six seconds of the stream's audio — commentary, compression and all. The stream can stay minimized; only its sound matters.

T+6.0 s

Match

The recording is fingerprinted and correlated against your file's soundtrack. In testing the peak lands within ~16 ms — even with a voice talking over the film at 1.2× its volume.

T+8 s

Play

VLC seeks to the matched moment plus exactly the time the search took, so it lands in sync — not eight seconds behind. Nudge buttons trim to taste.

watch parties · new

Host a session. Stream nothing but yourself.

For streamers who'd rather keep the film off the stream entirely: host a StreamSync session and broadcast just your camera and commentary. Every viewer plays their own copy of the film, verified against yours and locked to your timeline. What crosses the network is positions, clock ticks and one-way audio hashes — sign-bit fingerprints from which reconstructing even a second of the film is mathematically impossible.

Bring your own copy

Each viewer's file is checked against the host's hashes before anything plays. Different releases align automatically; a different cut is refused with a clear message — never mis-synced.

One clock for everyone

Both ends sync to internet time — PC clocks are routinely hundreds of milliseconds off — so "position X at time T" means the same instant on every machine in the room.

Pauses arrive with the words

Each viewer runs the host's timeline delayed by their own measured stream delay. When the host pauses to talk, your film pauses exactly as their voice reaches you.

what it does

Set it once, then just watch.

Works minimized

Audio-first sync never needs to see the stream's window — keep it in the background with the sound on.

Auto re-sync

A background check every 30 seconds corrects any drift past 0.35 s, quietly.

Follows pauses

When the film's audio vanishes from the stream, your copy pauses. When it comes back, everything resyncs by itself.

Facecam swap

While the film is paused, the stream's window comes forward so you watch the streamer talk — then the film takes back over.

Your VLC or ours

An embedded player with millisecond seeks, or your own VLC app driven remotely — your settings, your shortcuts.

Millisecond nudges

±0.1 s taps for final lip-sync, with an offset that's remembered on every future sync.

Heavy files welcome

4K HEVC, HDR, Dolby Vision — the audio matcher never decodes video, so big files sync just as fast.

Video fallback

Experimental screen matching with facecam ignore zones, for streams that bury the film's audio entirely.

Sessions at scale

One small relay serves thousands of watch-party viewers — rooms get unguessable codes, passwords optional.

Late joiners welcome

Join a session mid-film: your copy verifies, playback snaps to the live position, and you're in.
